Abstract

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) is a camera-based technique for measuring physiological signals, particularly cardiac activity. From the remotely measured signals, heart rate can be estimated, which is crucial for health monitoring. In this study, we investigate a driver health monitoring system based on remote heart rate estimation. However, driving environments represent uncontrolled settings where videos are subject to varying illumination conditions and frequent head movements. We introduce MS-rPPG, a multi-spectral framework that combines RGB with near-infrared (NIR) face video to alleviate rPPG estimation under challenging driving conditions. To combine the complementary features from two spectral videos, we propose a cross-spectral linear modulation (CSLM) strategy based on frequency-domain analysis. Moreover, we introduce MS-Mamba, a novel state space model designed to effectively model long-range temporal dependencies while jointly capturing cross-channel interactions between multi-spectral features. We collected a real-world dataset called MS-Drive, recorded from 50 participants while driving a vehicle. Experimental results indicate that MS-rPPG shows better robustness and heart rate estimation accuracy than previous methods, highlighting its promise for driver health monitoring.

🌈

Multi-spectral Input

Combines RGB and NIR facial video to leverage complementary spectral properties — RGB provides high-SNR pulse signals under good lighting; NIR is robust to illumination changes.

⚡

CSLM

Cross-Spectral Linear Modulation uses FFT-based physiological priors (40–200 bpm band) to adaptively modulate one spectral stream with scale and shift parameters from the other.

🔄

MS-Mamba

Multi-spectral Mamba block with temporal SSM scanning per modality, followed by bidirectional channel-axis scanning to capture inter-channel dependencies with linear complexity.

🚗

MS-Drive Dataset

Real-world driving dataset with 50 participants across Fitzpatrick skin types 2–6, with synchronized RGB, 850 nm NIR video, and ECG ground truth signals.
